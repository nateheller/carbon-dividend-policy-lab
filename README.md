# Carbon Dividend Policy Lab

Independent ISS Breakthrough 1 contribution by Nate Heller · Alberta pilot · November 2026

## GitHub Pages

Use `main` as the default branch and select **Settings → Pages → Source → GitHub Actions**. Then run **Deploy Carbon Dividend Policy Lab** from **Actions**. The deployment shows the public URL. Every push to main rebuilds the site. The weekday workflow refreshes the dated Bank of Canada rate snapshot. If the Bank is unavailable, the build can serve the bundled rates, and visitors who already have a newer snapshot keep it.

For a local static build, run `python check_pages.py` with Python 3.10 or later and Node.js installed. Serve the generated `dist` directory over HTTP to preview it. The model runs in the browser and downloads scenario results in CAD. Asset and data links are relative so the site works under the repository path.

## Run the demo

Extract the entire folder and double-click **Launch Lab.cmd** on Windows. It opens the lab in your default browser. Keep the launcher window open during use. You can also run **python app.py** from this folder and visit **http://127.0.0.1:8765/**. Use Python 3.10 or newer. The simulation and its bundled UN icons run offline with the Python standard library. External goal and source pages need internet access. The lab binds to this computer only. If port 8765 is busy, run **python app.py --port 8766**.

Change a policy rule to update the household comparison and emissions chart automatically. **Undo** restores the last applied policy and **Start over** restores the starting settings. **Explore the details** contains technical tables, national context, pinned inputs and methodology. The JSON download saves the scenario settings, input version, annual household results and emissions to JSON. Run **python -m unittest -v** to repeat the validation checks. Keep the downloaded scenario and the matching **inputs.json** together when archiving results.

**Display currency** starts in CAD. USD, EUR and GBP display the same results using dated [Bank of Canada daily averages](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/). The lab divides each CAD amount by the number of Canadian dollars per unit of the selected currency, then rounds for display. It tries to refresh the first time a foreign currency is selected and uses the newest saved or bundled rate when the Bank is unreachable. The bundled rates are dated November 13, 2026. The policy inputs, evidence, model and JSON downloads use CAD. Exchange-rate conversion is for display convenience only and does not measure purchasing power.

## Walkthrough

1. Read **How this works**. At the historical CAD 80 reference, compare **Share with everyone** and **Support lower-income households** for the lowest and highest income groups.
2. Change the second policy to **Share by household size**. Look at the result and its explanation, then compare payments across income groups.
3. Set the fuel response to zero. Charges and dividends still appear while emissions match the flat no-charge comparison. Restore 10% and compare the new revenue and emissions paths.
4. Read the live Global Goals commentary, then open **Explore the details** for sources and the fixed national context.

## Pilot choice

Alberta offers one jurisdiction with Statistics Canada household counts by provincial income quintile, reported household energy and spending, a documented historical consumer charge and rebate, and an official greenhouse gas inventory. Ontario shares the federal precedent and could be the next comparison with minimal changes to the design. British Columbia offers its own provincial carbon-tax precedent, which adds policy harmonization work. A multicountry launch would require harmonizing prices, coverage, fiscal eligibility, household units, purchasing power and emissions inventories. Alberta provides the strongest fit for a five-group teaching model by November 19.

The pilot compares universal and targeted eligibility. Select equal per person to explore universal payments or the two lowest household quintiles to explore targeting. The latter covers roughly 40% of Alberta households. This prototype addresses direct household costs, revenue recycling and conditional fuel response.

## Model design

**Observed or published inputs.** The versioned **inputs.json** pins the exact data used offline. Survey-derived counts and sizes, statutory rates, physical factors and historical inventory estimates are under **observed**. The federal target and independent national projections are under **external_projection**. To check provenance, open the sources above and compare the listed rows and units to the pinned values.

**Scenario assumptions.** Baseline gasoline use is 650, 1,150, 1,650, 2,100 and 2,600 litres per household per year. Natural gas use is 950, 1,500, 2,000, 2,450 and 2,850 m³. These are invented profiles ordered by income for demonstration. The apparent distribution pattern depends on that ordering. Counts and quantities are held flat in the no-charge case. The model applies full pass-through of the direct charge with no GST. The carbon price and recycling share hold steady from 2027 through 2030. The response is a user assumption about fuel quantities, scaled linearly with price and phased in by 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% over those years.

**Scenario calculations.** For each group and year, multiply baseline fuel quantities by one minus the assumed reduction. Multiply adjusted quantities by the corresponding charge rates, scaling from the CAD 80 reference. Multiply each group’s charge by its household count and sum to get total revenue. Multiply that total by the recycling share for the payout pool. Allocate using household count, estimated person count, or household count in the two lowest quintiles. Average group net equals dividend less charge. The weighted sum of net household impacts equals the negative of the unrecycled balance. Direct emissions equal adjusted volume times the applicable CO₂ factor and household count, summed and divided by one billion to convert kilograms to Mt. Internal calculations retain full floating-point precision and the display rounds.

**Interpretation.** A price with zero response shifts money while leaving direct CO₂ unchanged. Holding price, response and recycling fixed, changing who receives dividends reallocates payments without changing charge revenue or the emissions path. At the default price and response, the 2030 emissions reduction is 8% by construction. The simulation excludes foregone fuel spending or lost services from the net fiscal comparison. Separate national figures give progress context and cannot be updated by this pilot.

## Limits and next steps

- Replace hypothetical consumption with checked Alberta fuel volumes by income, or construct documented price-to-volume estimates with uncertainty ranges. Inspect suppression flags, household and tax-filer differences, timing, and differential use by rural status and tenure. Add income denominators only from an aligned household measure.
- Benchmark against PBO’s Alberta fiscal tables and ECCC historical receipts after reconciling years, fuel scope, GST and eligibility. Add business and indirect household costs only with suitable input-output data. Validate the revenue pool before expanding any province-wide interpretation.
- A causal extension would require an actual intervention, defensible comparison units, pre-trend checks, concurrent policy controls and sensitivity tests. The demo estimates no causal effects, poverty change, energy affordability, rebound, employment impacts, future adoption or global SDG attainment.
